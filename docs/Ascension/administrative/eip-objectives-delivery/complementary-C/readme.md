# Work with technical experts

:::subpages

## Extracted from the official PDF

This objective asks you to go and find an outside, qualified technical eye, to know how to formulate a precise question, to take a substantive critique, and to turn it into decisions. What is assessed is the approach: how you found the experts, how many you approached, how the exchanges were prepared, and the real effect on the project.

### Define what you want challenged

- 2 to 3 technical subjects identified to put forward: an architecture choice, a data model, a technology decision, a security approach, a scaling strategy, an API design.
- For each: what you are not sure about, what makes you hesitate, which options are still open.

### Find them and reach out

- Documented search: contributors to projects you use, authors of publications or conference talks on the subject, engineers and architects on professional networks, leads of technical communities, academics, alumni, professionals you met on placement or abroad.
- At least 6 experts approached, with dated evidence of sending.
- At least one follow-up for every approach left unanswered.
- An outreach register: who, what profile, through which channel you found them, when you contacted them, what message, what reply, what outcome.

### Prepare and run the exchanges

- A preparation pack sent to the expert at least 48 hours before: the project context in one page, the subject you are putting forward, the options you are considering, the precise questions you are asking. An expert who discovers the subject during the call can only give a general opinion.
- At least 2 actual exchanges, by video or in person.
- A written report within 48 hours: what was said, what was challenged, the recommendations received, any disagreements, the striking quotes.

### Turn it into decisions

- At least 2 concrete changes to the project coming out of these exchanges: a component reworked, a technology changed, a security fix, the data model adapted, an architecture choice revised, a tool or practice adopted.
- Traceability: for each change, which exchange it came from, what was changed, when, and the corresponding commit.
- At least one recommendation you did not follow, with the reason. An expert gives an opinion from their context, not yours: being able to say why you are not following it is part of the exercise.

### Take stock

An end-of-year memo: what the exchanges changed in the project, what you learned about your own way of designing things, and what you would do differently in how you reach out.

### Timeline and deliverables

| Item | Due | Deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Defining the subjects | Before end of December | Note on the 2 to 3 subjects to challenge, with the questions formulated |
| Outreach | January to April, 6 experts minimum | Dated outreach register, with how you found each one |
| First exchange | Before end of March | Preparation pack sent, written report |
| Second exchange | Before end of May | Preparation pack sent, written report |
| Changes from the exchanges | As you go | Table linking recommendations, decisions and commits |
| Summary memo | Before end of May | End-of-year memo |

### What you submit

- Note defining the subjects and the questions asked
- Complete dated outreach register, with how you found each expert
- Sample approach messages
- Preparation packs sent before each exchange
- Reports of the exchanges
- Table linking recommendations, decisions and commits
- Evidence of the recommendation you did not follow, and your reasoning
- Summary memo
